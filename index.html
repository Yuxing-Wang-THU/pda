Do predictions help when measurements are already available?

The requested ablation: keep current behavioral measurements and factual trial history, but remove directional predictions and their assessments. NoPred tests this directly. Facts and NoCheck test what later decisions gain from remembering predictions and receiving explicit assessments.

All variants retain module-level measurements and factual histories of bodies, edits, returns, and measured changes. They share initialization, controller budgets, retrieval, and selection rules. R denotes the common PDA-R ablation reference.

What the added ablations show

Make predictions, check them, and remember the results.

R has higher mean returns than NoPred, Facts, and NoCheck on both tasks. These comparisons support using predictions, assessments, and past records together; they do not assign the full gain to prediction alone.

Measurements and prediction targets help together.

R vs L

Keeping more of each past trial helps later decisions.

R has positive paired mean gains on both tasks. It adds past measurements, reasoning, predictions, and assessments together; the new ablations examine which records help.
